handbook / aspects

Purpose

Robert's aspect engine is a harmonic scanner. It walks the divisors of the circle from one to twelve, or sixteen, and tests every body pair against a window around each multiple of the base angle. A pair that matched under a smaller divisor is claimed and never tested again, so the classical aspect always wins over an exotic one. The same machinery, run three times with different offsets, finds the midpoint contacts.

The orb system

The default window of a divisor is a thirtieth of its base angle scaled by the global ORBIS-FAKTOR, twelve degrees for the conjunction, six for the opposition, four for the trine. Each body carries a percentage weight, the pair takes the LARGER of the two weights, and a weight of zero silences a body entirely. His alternative mode, the gleichwahrscheinliche Aspekte, swaps in a user table of orbs and restricts each divisor to its classical multiples, twelve keeps only 1, 5, 7 and 11, eight keeps the odd ones. His dialog explains the intent in his own words

...werden Orbes gewählt, die etwa GLEICHE Wahrscheinlichkeit für alle Aspekte mit Teiler 1 bis 12 aufweisen ! ( 1930 bis 2050 )
from the settings dialog of the equal probability mode

The comparison scan

a12asp compares two charts, the running sky over a radix or later two people, and it is stricter than the single scan. Divisors one to six with the fifth harmonic never scanned, the multiples with his skips so the square family tests ninety and two hundred seventy and the sixth only sixty and three hundred, the base orb pn over thirty regardless of the equal probability mode, and on the transit wheel the flat rule of one degree scaled by the radix body's weight. A near side guard keeps a pair from matching the same angle twice. The shell lists the hits under the transit view as running body, separation and radix body, the way his screen wrote T and R columns beside the wheel.

The Schiemenz counters

Two statistics ride inside the scanner, both introduced for the research correspondence his change log attributes to Schiemenz. The Triga counter joins conjunctions that share a body, consuming the shared ends so nothing counts twice. The grand trine counter searches triples of divisor three hits whose endpoints close a triangle, with a degeneracy guard against three bodies standing in one spot.

Midpoints

Three passes, direct, square and the semi square family, test every body against the half sum of every pair, each with the midpoint's antipode, one degree of base orb. A shared cube of flags spans the passes so the square that also fits the semi square family counts once. Two quirks are kept knowingly, the third body's weight only gates positivity in orbis_discr3, and a body standing at exactly zero degrees Aries never aspects at all because the original tests the raw position against zero. The tests document both.

The Aspektarium

aspar draws the whole scan as one triangular matrix and the shell keeps it as its own dialog. Exact separations in degrees and minutes stand above the diagonal, only for pairs the scan matched, the named aspect below it, the per body hit counts on the diagonal, beside it the divisor table with angle and orb and under it his planet weights. The naming runs through aspdis, ported as aspect_symbol, which folds the matched angle to the near side and divides it down through every divisor until a known aspect degree appears, the main aspects and the named minors get their glyph, everything else prints as divisor and rounded angle exactly like his DEFAULT branch. The maximal divisor choice of eight, twelve or sixteen rescans on the spot, sixteen only outside the equal probability orbs like his selection box, and the matrix folds its truncation quirks along, a five elevenths angle truncates onto the forty degree family just as his FIX did.

Verification

test_aspects.cpp runs synthetic charts through the scanner, the trine window, the conjunction across the zero wrap, the smaller divisor claiming a pair before a larger one can, the silent node pair, silent zero weight bodies, the orb factor scaling, the equal probability multiple filter, a chained conjunction for the Triga counter, a closed grand trine, midpoint hits in all three passes, the antipode rule and the cross pass duplicate guard. The aspdis transcription pins every named angle, the multiples folding back onto their divisors and the truncation onto CASE 22.
